GridGuide Demonstration Knowledge

GridGuide is a fictional electric utility demonstration. This source contains general guidance and fictional process information. It is not connected to a live utility.

Reporting a power interruption

GridGuide can create a report in a fictional demonstration system for one of these sample areas: North Ridge, Cedar Park, Riverbend, or Old Town. The resident chooses an area and a broad issue category: no power, partial power, flickering lights, or other. GridGuide repeats the selected area and issue, explains that the record is only for demonstration, and asks whether to create it now. GridGuide must wait for an unambiguous yes in a separate reply before submitting. The initial request to report an outage is not the final confirmation. If the resident says no or does not confirm, do not create the report.

A successful demo report returns a reference beginning with GG-. This confirms only that a fictional record was saved in GridGuide’s demonstration database. It is not sent to an electric utility, does not dispatch a crew, and is not proof that an outage is verified. Do not share account numbers, passwords, payment information, exact addresses, or other personal data in the demo flow.

If the demo action is unavailable or fails, GridGuide must say no report receipt was confirmed and offer a fictional contact option. Never make up a reference number.

Electrical hazards during an outage

Treat a fallen power line as dangerous. Never touch it or anything in contact with it. Keep away and do not drive through standing water where a fallen line may be present. If someone may have received an electric shock, contact emergency medical services. If there is immediate danger, contact local emergency services now; do not wait for GridGuide or a routine outage report.

GridGuide cannot assess a hazard remotely. Do not approach to inspect it, estimate a safe distance, or promise a response time. Report the hazard through the fictional contact options only after immediate safety needs are addressed.

Source basis: CDC, What to Do to Protect Yourself During a Power Outage.

Food safety during an outage

Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed as much as possible. U.S. FDA guidance says an unopened refrigerator generally keeps food cold for about four hours; a full freezer about 48 hours and a half-full freezer about 24 hours if kept closed. These are general estimates, not a guarantee for a particular appliance or food.

After power returns, use an appliance thermometer when available and follow current local food-safety guidance. Do not taste food to decide whether it is safe. When the temperature history is uncertain, consult the FDA source or a food-safety professional rather than relying on GridGuide to assess an individual item.

Source basis: U.S. FDA, Food and Water Safety During Power Outages and Floods.

Medical equipment and access needs during an outage

Some people rely on electricity for home medical devices. GridGuide does not assess medical risk, give treatment instructions, or enroll anyone in a priority-restoration program. Do not provide diagnosis, medication, or device details to this fictional demo agent.

If power loss creates immediate danger, contact local emergency services. Otherwise, contact the person’s healthcare provider and local emergency-management or utility support promptly to discuss an appropriate plan. The U.S. CDC recommends planning for backup power and alternative supplies for home-use medical equipment. Availability of utility registries or priority programs varies by provider and location; GridGuide cannot confirm eligibility.

Source basis: CDC, Power Sources.

Portable generator safety

Never operate a portable generator indoors or in a partially enclosed space such as a home, garage, basement, shed, or crawl space. Generator exhaust contains carbon monoxide, which cannot be seen or smelled and can be fatal. Keep generators outdoors and away from doors, windows, and vents, following the manufacturer’s instructions. Do not connect a generator directly to a home’s electrical system unless a qualified electrician has installed appropriate equipment.

If someone may have carbon-monoxide poisoning or is in immediate danger, move to safety if possible and contact local emergency services. GridGuide cannot diagnose exposure.

Source basis: FEMA, Use Generators Safely at Home; U.S. Fire Administration, Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Prevention.
